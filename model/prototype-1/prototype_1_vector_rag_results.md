### Overall Findings

The Vector RAG prototype performed well across the ten evaluated questions, achieving an average qualitative assessment score of **45.9/50 (91.8%)**. Generated responses generally answered questions directly, followed the requested structure and provided explanations consistent with the supplied evidence documents.

The strongest performance was observed for established information such as creatine loading protocols, strength improvements and supplement contamination risks. These responses contained relevant evidence and clear conclusions.

The main limitations involved evidence grounding, uncertainty and incomplete information. The creatine monohydrate versus HCl response introduced information not clearly supported by the retrieved documents. The long-distance running response also used relatively definitive language where more qualification was warranted.

The system frequently assigned high evidence confidence based on agreement between retrieved sources, although some documents contained overlapping information from the same organisations.

Overall, the prototype provides a promising baseline for evidence-grounded medical question answering. However, these grades are preliminary qualitative judgements rather than independently measured factual accuracy. Further evaluation should investigate unsupported claims, citation accuracy, handling of insufficient evidence and questions outside the available knowledge base.
